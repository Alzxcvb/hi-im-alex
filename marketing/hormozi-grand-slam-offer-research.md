# Hormozi Grand Slam Offer: landing page research

Compiled 2026-10-07 for the hiimalex.ai homepage rewrite ($300, one hour, 1:1 session, Claude installed and doing one real weekly task).

## Source key and reliability

- **PRIMARY (ACQ PDF)**: Acquisition.com's own free course checklists, fetched as PDFs from acquisition.com/files/. Strongest source here.
- **PRIMARY (transcript)**: Hormozi reading or speaking his own books on his podcast, via podscripts.co auto transcripts. His words, but machine transcribed (typos). Quotes were pulled by a fetch summarizer, so re-check any quote you plan to print.
- **PRIMARY (page)**: acquisition.com HTML pages.
- **Secondary**: third party summaries. Used only where noted.
- The course lesson pages (/training/offers4 to offers10) are video embeds. Only titles and the checklist PDFs are text, so the video content itself was not read.

## (a) CHECKLIST for the landing page

Each item can be checked against a page. Item numbers are referenced in the notes below.

**Value Equation**
1. The page names one dream outcome in the visitor's words (what they get, not what you do). Sources: S1, T3.
2. The page contains proof that raises believed likelihood: at least one named testimonial, case study, or concrete before/after. If none exists yet, the page uses a guarantee or a specific walkthrough instead and does not invent proof. Sources: S1, T3.
3. Time to result is stated as a concrete number (for example "in one hour", "by the end of the call"). Sources: S1.
4. Effort and sacrifice is lowered in writing: what the buyer does NOT have to do (no technical skill, no setup alone, no learning). Sources: S1, T3.
5. A visible "how it works" shows early progress (what is done by minute X). Sources: S1 ("How can we show progress?").

**Offer construction**
6. Problems/obstacles the buyer faces before, during, and after are each answered by a named item on the page (a "problem to solution" list). Sources: S2, T4.
7. Every stack item is a high value item the buyer would recognize as meaningful, fast, or easy. Items that are not high value are removed. Sources: S2.
8. The delivery is described by its format: 1:1, done with you or done for you, support after the call, live on video, speed of reply. Sources: S2 (Delivery Cube).
9. The page explains what the buyer gets after the call (support, follow up, next step), since "everything that happens before and after" is part of the offer. Sources: S2.

**Stack and price**
10. Each stack item has its own dollar value, and the value is justified in a short phrase. Sources: S3 ("Always ascribe a price tag to them and justify it").
11. Each bonus has its own name with a benefit in the title. Sources: S3, S5, T2.
12. Each bonus answers a specific objection or next problem (named on the page). Sources: S3, T2.
13. Bonuses, in total, are presented as worth more than the core. Sources: S3, T2.
14. The price is stated BEFORE the bonuses are introduced. Sources: S3.
15. Bonuses are used instead of discounts. The page never discounts the core price. Sources: S3, T2.
16. The page shows total stack value next to the actual price, and the dollar values are ones the owner can defend (see Honesty limits). Sources: S3, S4 (inferred; the exact "crossed out" layout is UNVERIFIED from primary sources).
17. The price is not the lowest in its category, and is high enough to feel serious. Sources: S4, T5.
18. Price is framed against value received (what the task is worth per week or per year), not against competitors. Sources: S4, T5.

**Scarcity, urgency**
19. Any scarcity is a true cap with the real reason shown (for example "I take N sessions per week because each is 1:1"). Sources: S6, T1.
20. Any urgency is a true date or event (for example a real cohort, a real bonus end date). No countdown that resets. Sources: S6, T1.
21. If a bonus is "going away", it really does go away on that date. Sources: S3, S6.

**Guarantee**
22. The page has a guarantee that names a result, a time, and a consequence ("If you do not get X in Y, we will Z"). Sources: S7, T2.
23. The guarantee has its own name. Sources: T2.
24. The guarantee is conditional only on things the buyer actually must do (terms match the activation points). Sources: S7.

**Naming and headline**
25. The headline uses at least three of the five MAGIC parts (reason why, avatar, goal, time interval, container word). Sources: S5, T6.
26. The avatar is named (small business owner, not technical). Sources: S5.
27. The offer container is a named thing, not "a consulting call". Sources: S5, T6.

**CTA and after the sale**
28. One CTA verb, repeated, asking for one action. Sources: T7.
29. The CTA is paired with a stated reason to act now. Sources: T8.
30. The headline promises something specific (a number, a time, a named result). Sources: T7.
31. After purchase, the buyer is offered a next step (upsell for the next problem), and the page or confirmation says what it is. Sources: T9.

## (b) Supporting notes

### 1. Value Equation
- Formula, spoken by Hormozi: "Dream outcome times perceived likelihood of achievement divided by Time delay times effort and sacrifice equals value." [T3] https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-3-100m-offers-book
- Acquisition.com checklist wording per lever: "Maximize Perceived Likelihood of Success (testimonials & proven case studies)", "Minimize Time to Success (How can we make this faster? How can we show progress?)", "Minimize Effort & Sacrifice (How can we make this easier? More Seamless? Convenient?)". [S1] https://www.acquisition.com/files/pricing-value-checklist.pdf
- On likelihood: "people pay for certainty." [T3] The plastic surgeon example (10,000th patient vs first) supports using proof.
- On time and effort: the London Underground example (a map showing when the next train comes reduced perceived waiting) and the liposuction line "Fall asleep, wake up thin, guaranteed" show perceived time and effort count. [T3]
- Offer copy implication: Claude install is low effort and fast by nature. The page should say so in numbers.

### 2. Grand Slam Offer creation
Checklist PDF steps: define the dream outcome; "List out Every Possible Problem they can Encounter" (every step, before and after the goal, "What next problem will they face"); list solutions; trim and stack. The graphic's tagline: "Make people an offer so good they would feel stupid saying no." [S2] https://www.acquisition.com/files/offer-creation-checklist.pdf
- Delivery Cube dimensions: group ratio, effort level (DIY/DWY/DFY), support, consumption type, speed and convenience, plus a "10x and 1/10th test". [S2]
- Trim and stack rules: "Remove all items that are NOT high value"; keep only a handful of high cost, high value items and the rest high value, low cost. [S2]
- Spoken: "I remove the ones that are the highest cost and lowest value first." [T4] https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-4-100m-offers-book

### 3. Bonuses
Bonus checklist, from Acquisition.com: [S3] https://www.acquisition.com/files/bonus-creation-checklist.pdf
- "Bonuses should be more valuable than core thing"
- "Present after price is said"
- "Stack bonuses instead of discounts to get them to say yes"
- "Bonuses should solve all perceived problems"
- "Give them a special name that has a benefit in the title"
- "Always ascribe a price tag to them and justify it"
- "Tools & checklists are better than additional trainings" (lower effort and time)
- "You can further enhance the value of your bonuses by adding scarcity and urgency to the bonus themselves"
- Tell them how it relates to their issue, what it is, what it does for them, and how it makes things faster, easier, or lower effort. Provide proof it is valuable.

Spoken versions: "The bonus should prove their belief incorrect." "The value of the bonuses should eclipse the value of the core offer." "Instead of offering a discount, add more value." [T2] https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-6-100m-offers-book

### 4. Pricing
- Checklist: "Be more expensive than everyone else (by enough that it causes consumer to pause)"; "Higher prices get people better results (and are therefore ethical)". [S1]
- Spoken: "don't compete on price"; "99% of businesses need to raise their prices to grow, not lower them."; "the moment the value they receive dips below what they are paying, they stop buying from you." [T5] https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-2-100m-offers-book
- NOT FOUND in primary sources: "price should be a fraction of value" verbatim (closest text: "we were charging only a fraction of what our clients made using our system", T5).
- NOT FOUND in primary sources: a crossed out total price layout and the exact phrase "no brainer". UNVERIFIED. Hormozi's frame is "feel stupid saying no" (S2). Secondary only: practitioner guides say a stated value near 2x price is not yet a Grand Slam Offer (secondary, https://www.autymate.com/book-summary/100m-offers, not fetched in full).

### 5. Naming (MAGIC)
- Acquisition.com checklist: "Make A Magnetic Reason Why / Announce Your Avatar / Give Them a Goal / Indicate a Time Interval / Complete with Container Word." Also: smaller time intervals for bonuses, and "Same bundle, different wrapper" (rename the same offer seasonally). [S5] https://www.acquisition.com/files/product-naming-checklist.pdf
- Spoken: "You don't need to use all five of them"; "make it as short as possible, but no shorter."; example "a free six week summer slim down." [T6] https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/m-a-g-i-c-offer-formula-ep-244

### 6. Scarcity and urgency
- Checklist types. Scarcity: cap on total clients, per week, or per class. Urgency: cohort based ("the next group starts next week"), seasonal, bonus or price based ("these bonuses are going away"), exploding opportunity. [S6] https://www.acquisition.com/files/scarcity-urgency-checklist.pdf
- Spoken: "The easiest scarcity strategy is honesty." "Just let them know your limits and let psychology do the rest." "Deadlines drive decisions." [T1] https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-5-100m-offers-book

### 7. Guarantees
From the Unbeatable Guarantee checklist: [S7] https://www.acquisition.com/files/unbeatable-guarantee-checklist.pdf
- Template: "If you don't achieve X in Y time we will..."
- Unconditional: no questions asked, with the line "Both decisions are risk free, but only one of them will help you achieve your goal."
- Conditional: "should always be 'better than money back'"; "match the guarantee terms with the activation points in your program". Examples: outsized refund (2 to 3x), keep working with them free, credit, 1:1 help until they reach it, delayed second payment.
- Anti guarantee: "All sales are final, best for exploding opportunity and high level services."
- Implied: performance or revenue share (only paid on results).
- Stacking: combine a conditional and an unconditional guarantee.
- Spoken: "Guarantees are the single greatest way to overcome the number one obstacle in sales, which is risk." "Name your guarantee something cool." [T2]

### 8. Free and attraction offers, upsells
- Spoken, Money Models recap: "Attraction offers get customers by offering something free or at a discount." "Upsells: whatever you offer next." "Downsell: whatever you offer after someone says no." Baseline: "a good money model makes more profit from a customer than it costs to get and service them in the first 30 days." [T9] https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-9-ten-years-in-ten-minutes-100m-money-models-audiobook (recap page only, not full chapters)
- Upsell timing: the transcript says "You offer a solution to the customer's next problem the moment they become aware of it." There is no explicit "right after purchase" rule in what was fetched. The Bonus checklist also says to solve "their next problem before they even encounter it" (S3).

### 9. Hooks, headline, CTA ($100M Leads)
- Hook: "The effectiveness of your hook is measured by the percentage of people who start consuming your content." Headline: "Try and include at least two" (of his listed hook elements). Numbers in a headline tell people what to expect. [T7] https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-4-post-free-content-100m-leads-book
- CTA: "only ask them to do one thing per call to action." [T7]
- Reason to act: "Then give them a reason why, using scarcity, urgency, and any other reasons you can think of." Examples: "If that sounds fair, book a call." "Our July 4th promotion ends Monday at midnight." [T8] https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/part-2-engage-your-leads-100m-leads-book

### 10. Acquisition.com's own pages as structure examples
- /training: headline "FREE ADVANCED TRAINING", one line subhead, a featured "BRAND NEW" card with a verb CTA ("Get My Custom Scaling Roadmap"), then cards each with a CTA and a short tag ("It's Free", "Live In-Person"), FAQ, long results disclaimer. https://www.acquisition.com/training
- /books: little copy, three covers, repeated single CTA "PURCHASE ON OUR STORE", no price, no stack, no guarantee. https://www.acquisition.com/books (so their own book page does NOT use the stack; it is not a Grand Slam example.)
- /training/offers: numbered module path and a results disclaimer ("not typical", "not a guarantee"). https://www.acquisition.com/training/offers
- Fetch note: the training page's FAQ ends "help you scale in 2024!", an out of date line. Do not copy their stale copy.
- shop.acquisition.com/products/100m-offers-hardcover returned HTTP 429, so the book sales page was NOT read. UNVERIFIED.

## (c) Honesty limits

What Hormozi says about real vs fake, and what the page can claim.

- "The easiest scarcity strategy is honesty." "Just let them know your limits." [T1]
- "It would be a lie to say that if you own a roofing business, you won't service them if they buy after the date." That is his own example of a fake deadline. [T1]
- On unethical use: "the unethical ways of using it, which I highly don't recommend long term. You'll lose money." [T1]
- "Never raise your prices without letting people know." [T1]
- Physical limits are the honest source: "as my time is limited by the laws of physics." A 1:1 hour has a real cap. [T1]
- Acquisition.com's own pages carry a results disclaimer ("not typical", "not a guarantee"). They are not guaranteeing outcomes while still using a guarantee on offers elsewhere. [page: https://www.acquisition.com/training/offers]
- Guarantee honesty: "To give a guarantee teeth, you have to decide what you'll do if they don't get the result." [T2] The page must state the refund terms and honor them.
- Value claims: Hormozi says to "ascribe a price tag and justify it" (S3). The checklist wants proof that the thing is valuable (S3). He does not set a rule about invented prices. Believable rule for Alex's page (my inference, not a Hormozi quote): only list a dollar value you can defend (what the task costs per hour, or what you would charge for the item alone), and do not list a "usual price" the item never sold at.
- Proof: likelihood is raised by "testimonials & proven case studies" (S1). Do not invent testimonials. If none exist, rely on the guarantee and a specific demonstration.

### Applied reading for the $300 Claude session (my inference)
- Honest scarcity available: a weekly cap on sessions (one person, one hour each). Honest urgency: only a real calendar date or a real bonus end.
- Candidate bonuses by the rules: a written one page recap of the weekly task (tool/checklist type, which his list favors over extra training); a follow up check in; a ready to reuse prompt sheet. Each needs a real price justification.
- Guarantee candidate (conditional, outsized or service type): "If Claude is not running your weekly task by the end of the call, I keep working with you for free until it is, or you get your $300 back." Terms must match what the owner does on the call.
